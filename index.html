How the 100m estimate works

Enter your run

Add the distance and time you recorded. Use Simple mode for a quick estimate.

Add the context

In Detailed mode, tell the app how you started and how the clock was triggered. A flying start gives you a run-up, while a standing start includes acceleration from rest.

Get your 100m estimate

Use the estimate to compare sprints across distances and track your progress.

Worked example: 60m in 7.50 seconds gives an estimated 100m time of 11.64 seconds.

In the app’s Track Meet mode, select Male, enter 60m and 7.50 seconds, and use a block start, electronic timing, track spikes, a dry rubber track, and zero wind. Leave age and altitude blank.

The 11.64-second result is a projection. An official 100m time requires a timed 100m race.

One way to use it

Put 40-yard dash times on a common scale.

A 4.50-second 40-yard dash averages 18.2 mph (29.3 km/h) over the full distance. Use the app’s 40-yard dash mode to get a 100m estimate that takes your timing method and conditions into account.

Save your sprint result as a transparent sticker and stamp it onto your own photo or video. Then share it to your Instagram Story or post it on TikTok. You can also save your result as a standalone video.
